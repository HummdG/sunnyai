What the assistant on Sunny.ai's own website answers from. Everything here is true
of Sunny or of solar in the UK generally. It names no installer's prices,

## How getting a quote and a survey works

1. You give your address, including the postcode.
2. Sunny measures your roof in 3D from public map and laser-scan data, and
   shows it to you.
3. Sunny asks a few questions: whether the roof it found looks right, shading,
   the type of property, whether you own it, and roughly how much electricity
   you use.
4. You get three indicative options, with the system size, expected
   generation and savings, and a price where the installer has published one.
5. If you would like to go ahead, you agree to be contacted and the installer
   arranges an on-site survey. The survey confirms the roof, the electrics and
   the final design, and the installer's written proposal follows it.

Every figure before the survey is an estimate. The survey is what makes it
firm.

## How Sunny measures your roof

Sunny finds the building at your address on OpenStreetMap and other open map
data, and measures its roof from Environment Agency LiDAR: laser-scan height
data flown over England. From that it works out each roof face: which way it
faces, how steep it is, and how much of it could take panels.

The measurement is indicative. The laser survey can be several years old, so a
recent extension may be missing, and shading from trees is not assessed. That
is why Sunny asks you to confirm what it found, and why the installer's survey
has the final word.

## Does solar work in the UK?

Yes. Solar panels make electricity from daylight, not heat, so they work on
cloudy days too, just less than in bright sun. A UK system makes most of its
electricity between spring and autumn, and much less in the short days of
winter.

The electricity is used in your home first. What you do not use at that moment
can be stored in a battery, if you have one, or exported to the grid.

## Which roofs suit solar

A roof facing south is best. Roofs facing east or west still work well and
produce somewhat less; a mix of east and west spreads generation across the
day. A north-facing roof is rarely worthwhile.

A pitch of around 30 to 40 degrees is ideal in the UK, though a wider range
works. Shading from trees, chimneys or nearby buildings reduces output, which is
why Sunny asks about it. Flat roofs can take panels on frames.

## Batteries

A battery stores the electricity your panels make during the day so you can use
it in the evening, when most homes use the most. With a time-of-use tariff, a
battery can also charge from the grid overnight at a cheaper rate. Whether a
battery pays for itself depends on how much you use in the evening and on your
tariff, which is what the quote options weigh up.

## Selling electricity back: the Smart Export Guarantee

The Smart Export Guarantee (SEG) has applied in Great Britain since January 2020. Electricity suppliers with 150,000 or more domestic customers must offer a
tariff that pays you for the electricity you export. The rate is set by each
supplier and must always be above zero. To qualify, the system must normally be
installed by an MCS-certified installer, and the export is measured by a smart
meter.

## MCS certification

MCS (the Microgeneration Certification Scheme) certifies installers and products
for small-scale renewable energy. An MCS-certified installation is usually
needed for the Smart Export Guarantee, and the MCS certificate is a useful
record when you sell your home.

## Planning permission

In England, solar panels on the roof of a house are usually permitted
development, so they do not need planning permission, as long as they do not
stick out more than 200 mm from the roof slope or rise above the highest part of
the roof. A listed building needs consent, and some conservation-area homes have
extra limits. The rules are similar but not identical in Scotland, Wales and
Northern Ireland. The installer checks this for your home.

## VAT on solar panels and batteries

Installing solar panels in a home is charged at 0% VAT in Great Britain until 31
March 2027. Batteries are included too, whether fitted with the panels or added
later. Northern Ireland has had the same relief since May 2023.

## Connecting to the grid

The installer tells your local electricity network operator about the system.
Most home systems are small enough simply to be notified once installed; larger
systems need the network operator's approval first. The installer handles this
paperwork.

## Looking after solar panels

Solar panels need very little maintenance. Rain keeps them reasonably clean, and
most systems come with an app that shows what they are generating, so a fall in
output is easy to spot. Panels typically last 25 years or more; the inverter,
which converts the panels' electricity for your home, usually needs replacing
once in that time.
